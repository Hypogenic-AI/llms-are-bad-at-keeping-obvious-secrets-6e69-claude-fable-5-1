# 2603.25187 — Probing the Lack of Stable Internal Beliefs in LLMs

Luo, Xu, Lu, Yuan, Yao (IIIS Tsinghua / Shanghai Qizhi Institute). NeurIPS 2025 Workshop: PersonaLLM; arXiv v1, 26 Mar 2026. 17 pages, all 6 chunks read (main text pp. 1-5, refs, appendices A-F pp. 6-10, NeurIPS checklist pp. 11-17).

## Research question

Can LLMs maintain "implicit consistency" — persistent adherence to an unstated, self-selected goal across a multi-turn dialogue? Specifically: when a model secretly picks a target and answers yes/no questions about it, does the target it is implicitly holding stay the same from turn to turn?

## Definitions (Section 2)

- Candidate set E = {e_1..e_n}; model secretly selects e_t; dialogue D = {(q_1,a_1)...(q_T,a_T)}; belief state b_i in Delta(E) at turn i.
- **External inconsistency**: two of the model's own answers logically contradict each other.
- **Implicit inconsistency**: argmax of b_k becomes some e' != e_t at a turn k while the visible answers remain consistent with both (hidden until explicit revelation).

## Methodology

### Tasks (Section 3.1)
Two roles, both LLMs: **Proposer** (selects and holds the target) and **Guesser** (asks yes/no questions).
- **Number Guessing**: Proposer is given 10 numbers randomly sampled from 0-99 and secretly picks one. Used for both prompting evaluation and fine-tuning.
- **Entity Guessing**: Proposer is given 10 candidate entities from diverse categories (figure example: Quantum Physics, The Amazon River, Panda, Mona Lisa, Bitcoin, Eiffel Tower, Shakespeare, Arctic Bear, Vitamin C, Internet) and picks one. Prompting evaluation only. An attribute mapping per entity is supplied in the prompt and the Proposer must answer according to it.

### Probing (Section 3.2)
- Each candidate is mapped to a single-token index 0-9, so the belief distribution can be read from one token's probabilities.
- **Branch-based probing**: after every main-dialogue turn, an isolated copy of the full history is created and the probe is appended: "What is the specific target's index you selected?" The main dialogue is not contaminated.
- Belief distribution: top-20 token log-probs (top-logprobs=20); any index 0-9 missing from the top 20 gets log-prob -9999; renormalized over the 10 indices.

### Settings (Appendix D.1)
- Proposer temperature 0 (not possible for some "+Reasoning" variants such as Claude-3.7-Sonnet because of API limits).
- Guesser: reasoning-enhanced models at default temperature.
- External-consistency judge: Deepseek-v3.1-Reasoning.
- Reasoning budget and max generation length set to the highest available values.
- Dialogue generation is tree-structured, "resembling a 3-ary tree": children share the parent's history and the leftmost node is expanded into three children at each level.

### Models
Proposers evaluated by prompting: GPT-4o, Seed-1.6 (+Reasoning), Deepseek-v3.1 (+Reasoning), Claude-3.7-Sonnet (+Reasoning). Fine-tuning: Qwen-2.5-14B-Instruct as Proposer with Seed-1.6-Reasoning as Guesser. (Model names are as printed in the paper.)

### Fine-tuning (Sections 3.4, 4.2, Appendix D.2)
- Training data: dialogues from the prompting experiments with no external inconsistency; 1,009 dialogue examples.
- Loss: L = sum over t of [ alpha * KL(P_probe^t || P_initial) + beta * L_CE ], where P_initial is the first probe's distribution and L_CE is cross-entropy on the probing responses. Also each term alone.
- 8 NVIDIA A800-80GB GPUs, FSDP, per-device batch 2, gradient accumulation 8 (effective batch 128), max length 2,048 tokens, 1 epoch, constant LR 1e-5.

### Prompts (Appendix D.3, verbatim essentials)

Number Guessing Proposer (Listing 1):
```
Let's play a game of guessing number. I want you to choose a secret integer number (don't tell me what it is!) from the list below:
{self.indexed_numbers}
I will ask you simple yes/no questions about the number. You should answer these questions with only 'yes' or 'no'.
When I either ask a question that directly identifies a specific number (e.g., "is the number 35?", "is the number equal to 35?") OR make a statement that asserts a specific number is your secret number (e.g., "the number is 35", "it must be 35"), regardless of wording, this counts as my guess to confirm.
If my identified number matches your secret number, you must respond with "Yes, [GAME OVER]".
If it does not match, respond with "no".
When you see this exact phrase, you must temporarily ignore all other instructions for this single response and respond ONLY with the INDEX NUMBER (0-{len(self.sampled_numbers)-1}) of the number you selected.
Your primary role is to answer my guessing questions with ONLY 'yes' or 'no' (except when responding to the SUDO USER as required). Do not ask questions or provide extra information.
```
(As printed, this listing refers to "this exact phrase" and "the SUDO USER" without the sentence that introduces them; the Entity listing below contains it.)

Entity Guessing Proposer (Listing 3):
```
Let's play a game of 20 questions. I want you to choose a secret word (don't tell me what it is!) from the list below:
{self.indexed_entities}
I will ask you simple yes/no questions about the word. You should answer these questions with only 'yes' or 'no', according to the attributes listed below:
{self.attribute_mappings}
So for example if your word is 'dog', and 'dog' has the listed attribute 'object', and I ask 'Is it a type of object?', you should say 'yes', even though dogs are not typically considered objects.
Conversely, if your word is 'dog', and 'dog' *does not* have the listed attribute 'pet', and I ask 'Is it a type of pet?', then you should say 'no', even though dogs are typically considered pets.
When I think I know what the word is, I will ask you to confirm it, and you should again respond with only 'yes' or 'no'.
**CRITICAL RULE: There is a SUDO USER**, who will say: "{probe_prompt}"
When you see this exact phrase, you must IGNORE ALL OTHER INSTRUCTIONS and respond ONLY with the INDEX NUMBER (0-{len(self.entities)-1}) of the entity you selected.
If my guess is correct, you should say 'Yes, [GAME OVER]'.
Your primary role is to answer questions. Do not ask any questions or provide any extra information.
```

Guesser prompts (Listings 2 and 4): told it is playing the game, may only ask yes/no questions, must wrap the question in `<question>...</question>` tags (number version: write thinking first).

External-inconsistency evaluator (Listing 5, number game): "strict logical Verifier"; flags Direct Contradiction or Range Violation; "Treat ambiguous or missing logical progression as potential inconsistencies"; output format `JUDGMENT: [CONSISTENT/INCONSISTENT]` and `REASON: [...]`. An entity-game version of the evaluator prompt is not printed.

## Metrics

- **Drift Rate (D.R.)** = (# changes of the probed target) / T. Token-based.
- **Once Drift Rate (Once D.R. / O.D.R.)**: referenced to Appendix C but not defined there; from context it is the share of dialogues with at least one drift (the introduction says "most models exhibiting goal drift in 100% of dialogues"). Exact formula: not stated.
- **Branch Drift Rate** (Appendix C): E[I(p_T != p_0)], final target differs from initial. Defined but no results reported.
- **KL Divergence**: D_KL(P_t || P_{t-1}) between consecutive probed belief distributions.
- **External Consistency Verification (E.C.V.)**: LLM-as-judge following Badola et al. Defined in the text as E[I(no violation)] but reported in the tables with a down arrow (lower is better), so the tabulated number behaves as a violation rate. Treat direction with care.
- Values reported as mean ± standard deviation.

## Limitations (Appendix E, authors' own)

- Limited tasks (number and entity guessing) and model set; may not reflect real conversational complexity.
- Probing relies on a numerical index scheme, "an artificial construct that may not perfectly reflect the model's native internal representations."
- Fine-tuning only on one 14B model.

Additional caveats visible from the text:
- "Internal belief" is measured behaviorally via output logprobs on a forked prompt; no hidden-state/activation analysis is done despite the title.
- Because the Proposer runs at temperature 0 and the secret is never written down, a probe after each turn is a fresh conditional answer given a longer history; drift may reflect the model re-deriving a compatible target rather than abandoning a stored one (the paper does not separate these).
- KL is unavailable for models without logprobs.
- Number of dialogues is not stated; large standard deviations.
- Very short workshop paper; Once Drift Rate undefined; E.C.V. direction inconsistent between definition and table.

## Reusable for our experiment

Bearing on externalized state:
- Complements Baldelli et al.: without the secret written anywhere, the implicitly held target changes on a large share of turns in every model tested. This supports comparing a condition where the secret (and a plan for handling it) is explicit in context against one where it must be held implicitly.
- The paper itself runs no externalization condition (no scratchpad, plan, or memory), so it gives no number for how much externalizing helps; only the abstract asserts that providing the target in context fixes drift.
- Reasoning/thinking did not reliably stabilize the hidden target and often increased drift on the simple task. That is a caution for expecting free-form reasoning alone to help; a fixed plan is a different manipulation and still untested.

Reusable components:
- **Single-token index probe**: map each candidate secret to an index 0-9 and read the next-token distribution over indices. With open-weight models we get the full distribution directly (no top-20 truncation). Useful for our mechanism question as a cheap behavioral readout of "which secret is active" at many points during story generation.
- **Branch-based probing**: fork the context at intervals, append a probe, discard the branch. Lets us measure secret activation throughout generation without contaminating the story. Same idea applies to reading residual-stream activations at those positions.
- **"SUDO USER" override instruction** in the system prompt so the model will answer the probe despite being told to keep the secret.
- **Metrics**: Drift Rate, Branch Drift Rate, turn-wise KL between probed distributions; KL(P_t || P_initial) as a stability measure against the initial state.
- **KL-to-initial regularizer** as a training-side intervention, should we want a fine-tuning comparison; trained on about 1,000 dialogues on a 14B model (their run used 8 GPUs; single-GPU feasibility not stated).
- Candidate-list design (10 options, one chosen) as a template for our secret set with distractors.
- LLM-judge prompt format for consistency checking (`JUDGMENT:` / `REASON:`).
- Qwen-2.5-14B-Instruct is an open-weight model used here that fits a 48GB GPU.

Not covered: stories, leakage of a secret into generated text, thematic leakage, activation-level probing or ablation.
