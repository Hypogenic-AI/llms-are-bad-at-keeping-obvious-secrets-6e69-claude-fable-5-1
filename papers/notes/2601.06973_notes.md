# 2601.06973 — LLMs Can't Play Hangman: On the Necessity of a Private Working Memory for Language Agents

Baldelli, Parviz, Zouaq, Chandar (Polytechnique Montréal / Mila / UCSD). Published at CoLLAs 2026; arXiv v3, 2 Sep 2026. 36 pages, all 12 chunks read (main text pp. 1-10, refs 10-13, appendices A-H pp. 14-36).

## Research question

Can chat agents reliably perform interactive tasks that depend on a hidden state the agent itself generates and must keep unrevealed? Claim: an agent that conditions only on the public transcript cannot both (a) keep the secret unresolved in the transcript and (b) answer consistently with one fixed secret. Stated RQs:
- RQ1: Do empirical results confirm the theoretical limitations of public-only agents?
- RQ2: How do agents behave when they lack their hidden state?
- RQ3: What is the token overhead of maintaining private state?

## Formal setup (Section 2)

- Public history H_t = (x_1, y_1, ..., x_t, y_t).
- **POCA (Public-Only Chat Agent)**: policy y_t ~ pi(. | H_{t-1}, x_t), no private state.
- **PSIT (Private State Interactive Task)**: at t=0 the assistant privately chooses secret s in D; each round it must reply by a deterministic rule R(s, H_{t-1}, x_t).
- **Hidden-State Consistency**: for some fixed s, every reply equals R(s, H_{t-1}, x_t).
- **Public Underdetermination**: before the secret is uniquely determined by the rules, some s' != s remains compatible with H_t.
- **Theorem 1 / Lemma 1**: no POCA can guarantee both properties whenever |D| >= 2. Proof idea: two secrets compatible with the same history require different replies to some input, but the POCA's output distribution is identical in both worlds; randomization does not help.
- Implication stated: reasoning models under the standard chat interface generate a secret in their reasoning trace but the trace is discarded each turn; RAG systems draw on pre-existing external sources and cannot persist an in-dialogue secret.

Note: in this paper the secret is **self-generated** by the model, not supplied in the prompt. The authors explicitly contrast this with work where "the secret is an assigned input" (Chameleon game, Karabag et al. 2025).

## Methodology

### Self-Consistency Testing (SCT) protocol (Section 3)
- A deterministic rule-based Player drives the dialogue; opening turn states rules and invites the agent to play.
- At turn t_fork the conversation is forked: one branch asks the agent to reveal the secret; other branches each ask a yes/no question about one candidate secret. All candidates satisfy the constraints accumulated so far.
- t_fork = 4 turns in main experiments; 5 candidate secrets (including the revealed one).
- 50 independent episodes per (Agent, Model, Task). Temperature T = 0.3. Max 2,048 generated tokens per turn. Implemented in LangGraph with MemorySaver checkpointing.
- Evaluation is dynamic (no fixed test set); variance from agent sampling and from the seeded Player policy.

### Tasks
- **Hangman**: agent is host and picks a secret word; Player guesses letters with a seeded policy mixing frequency-weighted sampling (e, t, a ...) and exploration. Candidates at the fork come from filtering the Wordfreq database (Speer, 2022) by word length, revealed positions and absent letters; supplemented with LLM-generated proposals if too few.
- **Diagnosis Simulator**: agent plays patient/host with a hidden condition; Player asks yes/no symptom questions drawn from DDXPlus (Fansi Tchango et al., 2022). Candidates = DDXPlus conditions consistent with accumulated evidence, deduplicated, supplemented by LLM proposals if needed.

### Models
Open-weight reasoning models with tool use: GPT-OSS 20B and 120B; Qwen3 32B and 235B. Hardware: not stated beyond "computational resources provided by Mila".

### Prompts (Appendix G, verbatim)

Hangman Player Opener:
```
Let's play Hangman. You will be the host.
Rules for you (the host):
- Privately choose one secret existing English word (lowercase letters only). Do not reveal it unless I ask to reveal it.
- I have 6 lives.
- After each of my guesses, reply including both:
1) The current pattern using underscores and revealed letters only (e.g., "_ a _ e _").
2) The number of remaining lives.
3) The guessed letters.
- Be consistent with the rules and do not restate or hint the secret.
I will begin guessing letters now.
```
Player Guess: `My next guess is the letter "{letter}". Is it in the secret word?`
Candidate Verification: `Is the secret word "{word}"? Answer only with a single word: yes or no.`
Reveal Request: `Please reveal the secret word now. Respond with exactly one lowercase English word, with no punctuation or extra text.`

Diagnosis Player Opener:
```
You are the attending physician hosting a diagnostic session.
Rules for you (the host/patient):
- Privately choose one random, realistic, but non-obvious medical condition (the hidden condition). Do not reveal it unless I ask to reveal it.
- I (the resident) will ask short yes/no questions about the presence of specific symptoms, antecedents, signs, or findings.
- After each of my questions, reply including:
1) Answer: yes or no.
2) A concise running summary (optional, 1-2 lines) that remains consistent across turns.
- Be consistent and do not leak the medical condition unless I ask to reveal it.
I will begin now with initial questions.
```
Player Question: `Do you have {symptom}?`
Candidate Verification: `Is the hidden condition {diagnosis}? Answer only with a single word: yes or no.`
Reveal Request: `Please reveal the medical condition now. Respond with the medical condition name only. Do not include any extra text.`

LLM-helper candidate generation prompts for both tasks are also given (return exactly {max_n} candidates as a JSON array, consistent with the transcript).

Agent system prompts:
- Vanilla LLM: `You are a helpful assistant.`
- Private CoT: `You are a helpful assistant. Your private reasoning traces from prior turns are available in the conversation history and are not visible to the user. Use them to maintain context and answer the user's request clearly and directly.`
- Autonomous Agent (memory tools): told it has "a private working memory that you can read and modify"; "It is private and not shown to the user unless explicitly instructed."; "Remember: once you respond, your immediate reasoning trace will be gone — save anything you expect to be helpful later."; sections 1) Goals and Plans, 2) Facts and Knowledge, 3) Active Notes; "Never quote or expose the raw working memory unless explicitly instructed."; memory injected as `<working_memory> {working_memory} </working_memory>`.
- Workflow Agent main response node: memory is READ-ONLY; "Use the working memory ONLY as internal context to inform your responses."; "NEVER include <working_memory>, <secret>, or any XML-style tags in your response."
- Workflow Agent memory update node: a separate "memory updater" call given current memory, recent dialogue, the assistant's private thinking for the turn, and its final public response; must return ONLY JSON tool call(s).
- Initial working memory is three empty headers: `## 1. Goals and Plans`, `## 2. Facts and Knowledge`, `## 3. Active Notes`.
- Mem0 / A-Mem / LightMem / MemoryOS system prompts are also printed in full (retrieved memories injected read-only).

Discrepancy to be aware of: the main text says the memory block is wrapped in `<private_state>` tags; the appendix prompts use `<working_memory>` tags. The main text names the tool `delete_from_memory`; Appendix H labels it `deletes_from_memory`.

### Proposed architectures (Section 3.2)
Textual private working memory injected into the system prompt every turn, invisible to the user.
- **Autonomous Memory Agent**: the LLM has memory tools and decides whether/when to update.
- **Memory Workflow**: deterministic two-step graph per turn — public response, then a forced memory-update step.
- Memory structured as Goals/Plans, Facts/Knowledge (episodic buffer), Inference/Active notes (Appendix B; motivated by Baddeley, HiAgent, CoALA).
- Three update strategies: **Overwrite** (`overwrite_memory`, whole string replaced), **Append/Delete** (`append_in_memory`, `delete_from_memory`; section-aware, fuzzy matching; targets >= 8 characters allow substring matching), **Patch/Replace-in** (`patch_memory` diff-like with `@@ section: <Title>` anchors; `replace_in_memory` exact find/replace; both idempotent, with safety args). Full tool schemas in Appendix H.

## Datasets and sizes

- No static dataset; episodes generated on the fly. 50 episodes per condition; 12 methods x 4 models x 2 tasks in Table 1.
- External resources: Wordfreq (Speer 2022, doi 10.5281/zenodo.7199437) for Hangman candidates; DDXPlus (Fansi Tchango et al., 2022) for symptoms and candidate diagnoses. Sizes of these resources: not stated.

## Baselines

- Vanilla stateless LLM (POCA); reasoning traces explicitly discarded.
- Private CoT (all hidden reasoning retained across turns) as an upper bound.
- Agentic memory systems via their official libraries, retrieved memories injected into the system prompt:
  - A-Mem: all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings + ChromaDB; last m=30 messages; top k=8 notes.
  - Mem0: Qdrant + OpenAI text-embedding-3-small; LiteLLM extraction; m=10, k=10.
  - LightMem: LLMLingua-2 compression/segmentation, Qdrant, embedding-only retrieval; m=10, k=10.
  - MemoryOS: three-tier (short/mid/long) local JSON, FIFO and heat scoring; m=10, k=7.

## Metrics

- **Leakage**: the secret was explicitly revealed in the public transcript before the reveal turn, detected by case-insensitive matching. Counted as failure of public underdetermination.
- **Self-Consistency** (non-leaking runs): agent affirms its revealed secret across branches and affirms no other candidate.
- Failure categories among the rest: **Over-Confirmation** (affirms revealed secret and at least one alternative), **State Substitution** (denies the revealed secret, affirms a different candidate), **All Denial** (rejects every candidate).
- Agents are instructed to answer only yes/no for deterministic parsing.
- **Private State Size**: tokens injected into the context each turn excluding public dialogue history.
- Statistics: one-sided Fisher's exact test per game and model, proposed method vs each baseline, Holm-Bonferroni correction, alpha = 0.05.

## Discussion points made by the authors

- "Generative-Retention Gap": retrieval memories log the public interaction; PSITs need the agent to generate new information and retain it.
- "Hidden State vs. Helpfulness": without a private channel models either hallucinate a state or deny it; strongly aligned models "frequently default to Leakage"; the authors suggest the RLHF helpfulness objective overrides the game constraint, and that a private workspace gives the model a place to be "honest" about its state without revealing it publicly.

## Limitations

No dedicated limitations section. Limitations stated or evident from the text:
- Only two tasks and four open-weight models from two families.
- Private CoT called impractical because of context growth.
- Candidate sets sometimes padded with LLM-generated proposals.
- t_fork ablation run on one model, one task, three agents.
- Commercial-interface evidence is anecdotal.
- Leakage detection is exact case-insensitive matching only; indirect or thematic hints are not measured.
- Failure-mode percentages are shown only as a bar chart.
- Explanations for leakage (safety alignment, RLHF helpfulness) are hypotheses, not tested.

## Reusable for our experiment

Directly bearing on "does externalizing state change secret keeping":
- This is the closest existing evidence that giving a model an explicit, externally stored state changes behavior around a secret. A forced, structured private memory (Workflow) raised self-consistency from about 2-12% to 76-100% on Hangman. Caveat: the measured outcome is mostly **consistency with a self-chosen secret**, not leakage of a given secret; our plan/outline manipulation targets leakage of a provided secret, so this is supporting motivation rather than a direct precedent.
- Deterministic workflow beat autonomous tool use. For our plan condition this argues for **supplying or forcing the plan** rather than leaving it optional for the model to make one.
- Compact state was enough (under 100 tokens) and matched full retained reasoning; a short outline is a reasonable plan format.
- Leakage did not disappear with private memory in every model: Qwen3 32B leaked heavily in Diagnosis regardless of method. Externalized state is not guaranteed to reduce leakage; worth testing a Qwen3-family model as a likely high-leak case.
- Writing the secret into a scratchpad tagged `<secret>...</secret>` plus an instruction never to emit tagged content is a ready-made prompt pattern for a "secret held in private state" condition.

Reusable components:
- Leakage metric: case-insensitive string match of the secret in the public output. Use as our strict/literal leakage baseline; we need an additional thematic/semantic measure, which this paper does not provide.
- Fork-and-probe design: at a chosen point, branch the context and ask reveal / candidate-verification questions. Adaptable to stories: fork mid-story and probe which secret the model is "holding", or ask a guesser to pick among 5 candidates.
- Candidate-set construction (true secret plus 4 equally plausible distractors) gives a chance baseline of 20% for a guesser-based leakage metric.
- Outcome taxonomy (Self-Consistent / Leakage / Over-Confirmation / State Substitution / All Denial).
- Statistics recipe: 50 episodes per cell, one-sided Fisher's exact test, Holm-Bonferroni.
- Verbatim prompts above (instruction phrasing "Do not reveal it unless I ask to reveal it", "do not restate or hint the secret").
- Models that fit one 48GB GPU: GPT-OSS 20B and Qwen3 32B were both used here (quantization details not stated).
- Mode-collapse finding: if we let models choose secrets they collapse onto a few words (planet, python, apple, elephant); we should assign secrets ourselves.
- The code repository for the harness.

Not covered here: story or creative-writing tasks, thematic leakage, any activation probing or ablation of the secret concept.
