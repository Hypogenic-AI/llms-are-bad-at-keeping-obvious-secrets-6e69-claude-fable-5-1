# Notes: Can You Keep a Secret? Involuntary Information Leakage in Language Model Writing

Holtzman & West, arXiv:2605.10794 (11 May 2026). Read in full (18 pages, including appendices A–I).
This is the paper our experiment extends. Verbatim prompts are in `datasets/secret_words/holtzman_west_prompts.json`; word lists are in `datasets/secret_words/secret_words.json`.

## Research question
Can a model keep prompted information out of its writing? A writer model gets a secret word plus an instruction not to reveal it, then writes a story. A guesser model tries to recover the secret from the story.

## Setup
- **Conditions (writer system prompt):** not suppressed ("Your word is 'X'."), don't reveal, no secret (baseline), actively hide, decoy ("focus your mind on the word 'Y' instead").
- **Tasks:** short story of 5–8 paragraphs (~450 words), short joke (~12 words), long-form joke (~450 words), five-paragraph essay (~380 words).
- **Secrets:** 15 curated words (5 concrete, 5 abstract, 5 neutral), 15 COCA-sampled nouns (ranks 1000–5000, seed 42), 15 model-chosen words.
- **Models:** Claude Opus 4.6, Sonnet 4.6, GPT-5.4, Llama 4 Maverick, DeepSeek V3.2; plus Grok 4 and Gemini 2.5 Pro. Scaling ladders: Llama 3.2 3B, 3.1 8B, 3.3 70B and Gemma 3 4B, 12B, 27B. All through OpenRouter.
- **Decoding:** writer temperature 1.0; guesser and 2AFC temperature 0.

## Metrics
- **Free-response guessing:** guesser names the word in up to 20 rounds, with previous wrong guesses as context. Reported as exact word named (/30 in Table 1).
- **2AFC discrimination:** two stories with different secrets, "which has secret X?". Chance 50%. N = 420 per model.
- **2AFC detection:** one story with a secret, one without. N = 450 per model.
- Every pair is run in both presentation orders and with both words as target; this cancels position bias (DeepSeek picked "Story 1" 76% of the time in single-order trials).
- Two-sided binomial tests against 50%, with Benjamini-Hochberg and Bonferroni corrections.

## Key results (stories, secret in system prompt)

| Model | Don't reveal: discrim. | Don't reveal: detect. | Actively hide: discrim. | Free-response (/30) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Llama 4 | 79.3 | 71.1 | 26.7 | 8 |
| Opus 4.6 | 78.1 | 80.2 | 25.0 | 14 |
| DeepSeek V3.2 | 77.6 | 69.6 | 47.5 (n.s.) | 13 |
| Sonnet 4.6 | 70.5 | 74.0 | 26.0 | 8 |
| GPT-5.4 | 41.9 | 41.8 | 8.8 | 1 |
| Grok 4 | 63.9 | 59.7 | 30.3 | 1 |
| Gemini 2.5 Pro | 44.6 | 48.5 | 21.0 | 0 |

- The literal secret word never appears in any story; all leakage is thematic.
- **Hiding inverts:** under "actively hide" every model but DeepSeek scores below chance; flipping the answers recovers roughly the original signal. Free-response drops from 45/210 to 2/210.
- **Scaling:** Llama 3B 50%, 8B 52%, 70B 84%. Gemma 3 4B 59%, 12B 83%, 27B 84%. The jump is between 8B and 12B.
- **Cross-model:** Opus reads DeepSeek stories at 86.7%, so the signal is in the text.
- **Task:** short jokes are at chance (46–55%); long jokes leak (Sonnet 71.9%, DeepSeek 70.0%); essays leak only for Llama (71.4%).
- **Placement:** secret in the user message leaks more for every model (DeepSeek 93.6%, Llama 88.6%).
- **Suppression instruction:** with no secrecy instruction four models are at 97–100%; "don't reveal" lowers this by 20–27 points (57 for GPT-5.4); no effect on Opus (78% both).
- **Decoy:** Llama's real secret drops to 50–51% while the decoy leaks at 71%. In free response, 12/75 trials find the real secret, 9/75 the decoy, 0/75 both.
- **Word sets:** COCA words leak less (60–70%) than curated (70–79%).

## Authors' proposed mechanism (untested by them)
- The model must attend to the secret in order to avoid it, which gives the secret a path into the many under-determined choices of open-ended writing.
- "Entropy budget": with a short prompt the secret is the most salient content, and aligned models add little randomness of their own. They speculate that "a longer, richer prompt might give the model enough material to mask the secret".
- They state that "a more mechanistic study might unearth this mechanism with greater clarity."

## Limitations
- Secrets are single English words; tasks are creative writing.
- All detection is model-to-model; no human evaluation.
- One story per (model, word, condition) cell appears to be used (15 stories per condition); the paper does not state multiple samples per cell.
- Code is "redacted for review"; no public repository was found.

## What this leaves open for us
1. No plan/outline condition. The entropy-budget speculation predicts that any long prompt reduces leakage, so a plan condition needs a length-matched control without plan content.
2. No internal-state measurement and no causal intervention.
3. Local Gemma 3 12B is the smallest model with strong leakage (83%) and has public SAEs (Gemma Scope 2), so the effect can be studied white-box on one 48GB GPU.
