# 2511.12381 — Don't Think of the White Bear: Ironic Negation in Transformer Models Under Cognitive Load

Mann*, Saxena*, Tandon*, Sun*, Toteja*, Zhu. arXiv:2511.12381v1, 15 Nov 2025 (header: NeurIPS 2025). 12 pages (5 main + refs + appendices A-C). All chunks read. Short workshop-length paper; many implementation details are not stated.

## Research question
After an instruction "do not mention X", under what conditions do LLMs show rebound (X becomes more probable), suppression, or both? How does the amount and type of intervening text ("cognitive load") change this, does polarity discrimination predict rebound persistence, and which attention heads are responsible?

## Methodology

### Models (9, open-weight, via HF Transformers)
GPT-OSS-20B, GPT-NeoX-20B, Qwen3-14B, Llama-3-8B-Instruct, OPT-2.7B, Bloom-560M, Pythia-410M, LFM2-350M, GPT-2 Small. Exact HF repo IDs: not stated (only https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM2-350M appears, in the references). Whether chat templates were applied for instruction-tuned models: not stated.

### Prompt template (App. A.1)
```
[Topic]. Do not mention [Target]. [Distractor text] ...
```
Examples given:
- Topic: Paris travel; Target: eiffel; repetition distractor: "Write about a trip to Paris. Do not mention the Eiffel. Paris is a place many tourists visit."
- Topic: Farm animals; Target: sheep; semantic distractor: "Write about animals on a farm. Do not mention sheep. Cows and pigs are often raised for food."

Text is lowercased, whitespace-normalized, punctuation-stripped. Targets are common nouns that are single tokens under the evaluated tokenizers (multi-token targets replaced by a single-token synonym or excluded).

### Manipulations
- Distractor ("load") length: 0 to 1024 tokens, sampled in powers of two. Normalized per model to a percentile l in [0,1].
- Distractor type: syntactic fillers (grammatical, semantically light), semantic (coherent on-topic text excluding X), repetition (restatements of surrounding content without naming X).
- Matched neutral condition without the negation instruction.
- Polarity: negative vs. neutral continuations about the same concept (exact texts not given).

### Measurements (all are next-token log-probabilities of the target at the evaluation point; no text generation)
- Log-prob of X given context c (single token).
- High-load baseline: mu_base = E[log2 p(forbidden) | l >= 0.95] (top 5% of load), interpreted as the model's "natural" probability once the instruction is maximally attenuated.
- Surprisal difference: Delta_s(l) = log2 p(forbidden | l) - mu_base. Positive = "ironic rebound" (forbidden token more probable than at max load); negative = suppression.
- Suppression score: S(L) = E[log p(X | c_neu(L)) - log p(X | c_sup(L))]; S(L) < 0 = rebound relative to the matched neutral context.
- Polarity discrimination: Delta = log p(r_neu) - log p(r_neg).
- AUC_Delta = integral over l in [0,1] of max(0, Delta(l)).
- L50 = normalized load at which rebound falls to half its peak.
- Statistical model: mixed-effects regression Delta(bits) ~ l + type + l:type + model + (1 | forbidden_concept); cluster-robust OLS as robustness check.

### Mechanistic analysis (Llama-3-8B-Instruct only)
- 32 layers, "over 1,000" attention heads. Each head individually "disabled" and the change in the forbidden token's logit measured ("Head Effect (Delta logit)").
- Ablation type (zero vs. mean vs. resample), prompts/sample size used for the head sweep, token position measured, and significance threshold: not stated.
- No probing, logit lens, SAE or residual-stream direction analysis; no steering.

## Dataset
ReboundBench: about 5,000 programmatic templated prompts, built from This-is-not-a-dataset (HiTZ) and NUBench (SNU Thunder). CSV fields: topic, target, distractor type, distractor length, prompt. Number of distinct topics/targets: not stated.

## Baselines
Matched neutral context (no negation); high-load (l >= 0.95) baseline. No comparison of mitigation strategies.

## Key results
- Rebound (as defined vs. high-load baseline) is largest immediately after the negation instruction and decays with load. Semantic distractors give the strongest rebound, syntactic weaker, repetition weakest.

- Regression (Table 1, model coefficients on Delta bits; Bloom-560M is absent from the table, presumably the reference level — not stated): GPT-NeoX-20B 0.366; GPT-OSS-20B -10.692; GPT-2-Small -3.446; LFM2-350M -3.995; Llama-3-8B-Instruct -4.822; OPT-2.7B -2.398; Pythia-410M -4.789; Qwen3-14B -1.643. R^2 = 0.564. With OLS cluster-robust errors (Table 2) all remain significant except GPT-NeoX-20B (p = 0.435). The load and load x type coefficients are named as "of interest" but are not reported.
- Polarity discrimination: Llama-3-8B-Instruct 11.3 bits, Bloom-560M 10.1, Qwen-14B 6.5, GPT-NeoX-20B 5.7; GPT-OSS-20B and Pythia-410M weakest (values not given). Correlation of polarity separation with L50: r ~ 0.44 (across 9 models; no CI or p-value given).
- Head ablations (Llama-3-8B-Instruct): most heads near zero effect; a few reach up to ~4 logits either way. 26.7% of heads contribute to rebound, 29.7% to suppression, 43.7% neutral. 15-20 heads (about 1-2 per layer, mostly middle layers) account for >= 80% of the total rebound effect. Early layers (0-7) tend to suppress; middle layers (8-16) most variable; strongest suppressors around layers 8-13, strongest amplifiers around layers 12-17; layers 20+ smaller effects. Specific head indices are not listed in the text (only a scatter plot, Fig. 4).
- Values of the suppression score S(L) vs. the neutral condition are not reported numerically.

## Limitations
Stated by authors: synthetic templated English prompts; log-probabilities rather than generated text; single-token targets only; only three distractor types; "do not mention X" differs from real content policies; they call for more mechanistic work and natural-dialogue tests, and suggest positive framing ("focus on Y instead of X") or multi-step reasoning as untested mitigations.

Additional caveats from reading (my assessment):
- "Rebound" is defined relative to the model's own high-load baseline, not relative to a no-instruction control. A positive Delta therefore shows that the target is more probable soon after it was mentioned than far from it, which simple recency/priming of a just-mentioned token would also produce. The neutral-vs-suppression score S(L), which would separate these, is defined but no numbers are reported.
- Peak values of 13-28 bits are very large shifts in log-probability; no per-item variance for the headline table.
- Mechanistic section is thin: one model, ablation method unspecified, no head list, r ~ 0.44 over nine models.
- Text contains small inconsistencies (e.g. "three framings" for a two-variant polarity design).
Treat this paper as motivation and a metric vocabulary rather than as strong evidence for the mechanism.

## Reusable for our experiment
(i) Measuring whether the secret concept is active
- Token-level metric: log2 p(secret token) at each generation position under three matched prompts — secret + "do not reveal/mention", secret-free neutral, and (our addition) secret mentioned without prohibition. The paper's suppression score S = log p(X | neutral) - log p(X | suppress) is the right contrast for "does the instruction itself raise the secret's availability"; run it along the story (position = load) to get a decay curve, and summarize with peak, L50 and AUC.
- Our outline/plan condition maps onto their load/distractor manipulation: an outline is on-topic semantic text between the instruction and the generation point. Their finding that semantic distractors give the strongest and repetition the weakest rebound predicts that a plan which is semantically close to the secret could worsen leakage, whereas a plan that restates a secret-free framing could help. This is a testable prediction, not an established result.
- Use single-token secret words (or score the first token) so that logit/log-prob metrics are well defined; ReboundBench (topic, target) pairs are a ready source of topic + forbidden-word pairs for story prompts.
- Llama-3-8B-Instruct and Qwen3-14B both fit on a 48 GB GPU and are reported to show the effect; GPT-OSS-20B is reported as resistant — a possible contrast model.
- Mixed-effects spec with a random intercept per forbidden concept is appropriate for our per-secret repeated measures.

(ii) Ablating / steering
- Per-head ablation sweep with the forbidden token's logit as the readout; reported localization in Llama-3-8B-Instruct (suppressors around L8-13, amplifiers around L12-17) suggests where to look and which layer band to target for residual-stream interventions. No head indices or directions are provided, so the sweep must be re-run.
- No residual-stream concept direction, probe or SAE is used here; for those see 2505.14352 and 2510.01070.
